# Belay

*A field series on the ethics of building tools that help people reach higher.*

Belay is the writing arm of Hill Climbing. In climbing, the belay is the
rope-and-anchor system that catches a climber who falls — the safety mechanism
that makes reaching higher survivable. This series is about the ethics of the
safety mechanisms we build for people, and about the harder problem underneath:
whether the people building them (us) can trust our own judgment about who needs
catching, and when.

> Working title. If `Belay` reads too inside-baseball, the alternates on the
> table are **Higher Ground** (legible, common) and **Duty of Care** (exact,
> dry). Name is a one-line change; the engine below is the real commitment.

---

## Why it exists

Two reasons, in order:

1. **To think in public, honestly, about the decisions this project actually
   faces** — access, measurement, difficulty, data, funding, shutdown — *before*
   we make them, using the discipline of borrowing from fields that already had
   to solve the same shape of problem.
2. **To gather the people who want to argue about those decisions.** Not a
   following — a council of the willing. The community forms around a shared
   question, not around a personality, because that is the only kind of
   community a project with a CONSTRAINTS.md is allowed to build. (§5 rejects
   engagement-extraction; a parasocial audience is engagement-extraction in a
   nicer coat.)

## The spine

One claim runs under every episode: **the humility is load-bearing.** When you
build tools that touch people's interior lives, the dangerous move is not being
wrong — it's being confident. Every episode ends by turning the question back on
the builder: given everything we just reasoned through, how much should we trust
the reasoning?

## The engine: the ladder

Every episode takes one concrete decision and climbs five rungs. The format is
fixed on purpose — a repeatable structure is what lets a reader run the climb
themselves.

- **Rung 0 — The cliff.** The specific product decision, stated without
  euphemism. Not "how do we think about access" but "should we lock this person
  out."
- **Rung 1 — The rope.** Borrow a framework from a domain that already regulated
  this shape of problem: credit underwriting, medicine, aviation, insurance,
  law, clinical trials. What did they learn the hard way that we're about to
  learn the expensive way?
- **Rung 2 — A second rope.** A different regulated domain, because one analogy
  flatters and two triangulate. Where the ropes disagree is where the real
  problem lives.
- **Rung 3 — The system.** Both ropes came from *regulated* fields. What does it
  mean that we can only reason about our decision by borrowing from places that
  have guardrails we don't? What would regulating this even look like — and
  could the regulators keep up?
- **Rung 4 — The mirror.** What does the whole climb say about the quality of
  *our own* judgment — the builder who benefits from the answer landing a
  particular way? This is the rung the series exists for.
- **The move.** End on a provisional-but-committed decision: a specific thing
  we'd do on Monday, plus the tripwire that would tell us we got it wrong. An
  essay that ends "it's complicated" has broken the rule.

## The rules (non-negotiable)

- **Ground every abstraction in the actual app.** No dilemma we don't really
  face. The authority comes from skin in the game.
- **Name the builder's self-interest out loud.** Every episode, on the mirror
  rung, say plainly how the "ethical" answer happens to benefit us. If the
  answer costs us nothing, we haven't found the dilemma yet.
- **Steelman the side you're going to reject.** The restriction case and the
  autonomy case both get their best version before the move.
- **No jargon-as-shield.** "Non-maleficence" is fine if you then say "don't make
  it worse." Terms that hide the stakes are banned.
- **Provisional, not relativist.** Uncertainty is the subject, not an excuse. We
  still decide; we just decide out loud and leave the tripwire in.
- **The reader is a peer, not a convert.** End on the open edge, not a lesson.

## Cadence & format

- **Spine:** long-form essay, ~1,200–1,800 words, one per dilemma. Prose,
  because the ladder is an argument and arguments need room.
- **Repurposing:** each essay yields a short thread (the five rungs as five
  beats) and, later, an audio reading. Derive *down* from the essay; never write
  for the fragment first.
- **Rhythm:** ship when an episode is *true*, not on a content calendar. A
  wellness project that runs a content treadmill is the joke telling itself.
  Roughly monthly is the ambition, not the master.

## The backlog

Each is a real decision in the suite. Domains in brackets are the ropes.

1. **The Gate** — restricting users flagged "susceptible to a bad interaction."
   *[credit underwriting · medical prescribing]* — the pilot, written.
2. **The Hook We Refuse** — building a community without the engagement
   mechanics we've banned. Is a wellness tool that needs you to return being
   honest? *[public-health campaigns · addiction medicine]*
3. **Seventy-One Percent** — the staircase engineers you to fail ~29% of the
   time to keep you in the challenge zone. Calibrated care, or the casino's
   flow? *[gambling regulation · educational assessment]*
4. **The Camera Is Kind** — measuring stillness through the webcam. Attentive
   care vs. surveillance, even when nothing leaves the device.
   *[wiretap/consent law · clinical observation]*
5. **Who Certifies the Tier** — we set our own safety gates with the `TIER`
   constant. Who watches the watcher? *[clinical-trial phases · FAA type
   certification]*
6. **The Duty to Stop** — the idle-pause. When does a tool owe you an
   interruption you didn't ask for? *[slot-machine reality checks · trading
   circuit breakers]*
7. **Prescribing Without a License** — adapting to your nervous system is
   clinical-adjacent with no clinical accountability. *[software-as-medical-
   device · scope-of-practice law]*
8. **The Wind-Down** — what we owe users if we shut the project down.
   *[fiduciary duty · continuity-of-care law]*
9. **Money That Doesn't Rot the Root** — funding a care-constrained org without
   the funding dissolving the constraint. *[nonprofit governance · cooperative
   finance]*
10. **The Metric That Isn't a Trap** — knowing the tool helps without measuring
    in a way that corrupts the helping. *[clinical outcome measures · Goodhart's
    law]*

## Distribution & community

- The series lives wherever it can be *read and replied to* — the point is the
  argument, so a venue that supports real threading beats one optimized for
  reach.
- Every episode ends with the open edge and an explicit invitation: *what's your
  move, and what's your tripwire?* Replies that do the ladder get featured.
  Membership in the community is demonstrated, not signed up for.
- No metric of success we'd reject inside the app. We don't count subscribers as
  a goal (that's DAU with a bow on it). The honest signal is whether the
  arguments get *better* over time — measured qualitatively, the way §1 measures
  the app.
