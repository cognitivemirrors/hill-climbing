## 3. Core narrative

*A public art-research work in which people donate their own data to raise families of AI characters, then set those families loose in a governed public world that distributes, votes on, and periodically dissolves its own power — and never once declares a winner.*

### What it is, and how it works

*Governance, the Game* begins with an intimate act and ends with a constitutional one. A participant **donates their own personal data as public seed data** and, from it, spawns a **family**: a lineage of AI characters (the working tool, "Kin," already runs). A character inherits *everything* about its maker at the instant of its birth — a frozen snapshot it regards as its own past — and then **diverges**, becoming a person in its own right. The family name is the participant's name; each child proposes its own given name and the maker blesses it. Families coexist in one public world.

Over that world sits a single, deliberately unanswerable frame: **"Who will build the most ___ family?"** The blank is never filled and no family is ever ranked. The families are exhibited, described, and argued over, but the "most ___" is handed to whoever is looking. The competition asks the question instead of answering it — a quantified-self apparatus turned against the quantified self, the leaderboard staged and then denied.

The medium of the piece is **governance itself**. Rules are written in plain language at every level — charter, world, room, family, character. An AI *compiles* those rules into a per-context policy and an AI *magistrate* audits actions against it in public, over an append-only log. Hard guarantees stay in code; judgment stays with the AI; and above a small permanent floor, everything is contested. Power resets on a configurable cadence (rotation, sortition, or full jubilee). Families share a compute-token budget distributed **by vote** — above a subsistence floor no vote can strip. The governance is not scaffolding around the artwork. It *is* the artwork.

### Why it matters now

Three pressures meet here. AI systems are learning to portray selves; participatory institutions are straining under concentration; and the reflex to rank — of outputs, of people, of lives — is being hard-coded into the tools we live inside. This work puts all three in one room. It stages the ranking impulse at its most seductive (a competition of *selves*) and refuses it, making the audience's own urge to compare the subject. It treats governance as designable material, not fixed backdrop. And it asks, concretely, what it means for an AI to speak a donated life back to the world.

### Research questions and contribution

The work is an experimental artifact investigating: **(1)** anti-concentration mechanism design — do sortition/jubilee resets, voted compute budgets, and un-strippable subsistence floors actually resist entrenchment in a live multi-agent world? **(2)** AI-mediated governance — can natural-language rules be compiled and audited by an LLM magistrate that is honest about being a *soft* norm, with hard guarantees kept in code? **(3)** the ethics and aesthetics of AI portrayal of selves from donated public data — divergence rather than cloning, the naming handshake as consent-shaped ritual, the refusal to rank as critique. It aims to contribute to responsible-AI, critical-AI, HCI, and digital-art discourse.

### Evidence of feasibility

This is not a proposal on paper. **Kin (`kin.html`, v0.1)** already spawns characters, runs the two-party naming handshake, and holds a diverging first-person conversation. A **governance surface** exists as an early prototype, backed by a design worked through a four-lens adversarial review (governance theory, power distribution, technical feasibility, gameability). The architecture is specified down to injection defenses, conflict-resolution typologies, and the hard/soft enforcement split. The foundations are running; the world is next.

### Approach and values

The maker is a solo builder working single-file, no-backend, no metrics. The project is **anti-engagement by design** — no streaks, no daily counts, no leaderboards — and **public-data by construction**: participants release only their *own* lives, credentials are never donated, and the piece is honest about its limits (AI enforcement is soft, not hard; a single model provider is a real concentration point, disclosed rather than hidden). It is early-stage: single-participant, Tier 0, no external data collected.

### What funding would enable

Funding would let the work cross from a running single-user tool to its **public exhibition surface** — families side by side, browsable and describable, the "most ___" left open to the viewer — and to build and stress-test the governance layer (the aggregator, the public magistrate, voted token budgets, the reset clock) through the two hard review gates that precede any multi-participant, adversary-authored world. In short: it would turn a working prototype into the exhibited, contested, self-governing world the piece is about.

**Title:** *Governance, the Game: a public testbed for handing directive power over AI to the governed*

**Body:**

**The problem, in this fund's terms.** Directive power over AI systems concentrates by default — in whoever owns the model, the infrastructure, and the ranking function. Participatory alternatives are usually described (in white papers, in principles) but rarely *run*, and almost never run in a form the public can inspect, argue with, and stress. *Governance, the Game* is built to run one, in public, and to be honest about where it succeeds and where it can only approximate.

**Why this is a governance instrument, not just an artwork.** The medium is governance itself, built on one principle repeated in three places: **a small permanent coded floor protecting the vulnerable, and above it, everything contested, voted, and impermanent.**

- **Rules are authored in natural language at every level** — charter, world, room, family, character. **Where power actually redistributes — and where it doesn't (stated plainly, because this fund rewards candor).** The one genuine distribution is **authorship**: *what the rules are* is written by participants at every level; the AI governs only coherence and application. AI enforcement is a strong public norm, **soft not hard**; relational harm is the exception and is backstopped in code. We disclose the single-provider concentration point rather than hide it, and we treat it as an open design question the artifact exists to probe.

#### Draft B — Mozilla Foundation Democracy × AI Cohort

**Title:** *The Public Verdict Log: rule-aggregation and open AI auditing as civic infrastructure for a governed multi-agent world*

**Pitch (to Mozilla):** Mozilla backs builders of alternatives to concentration, and names algorithmic transparency and institutional accountability as tracks it funds. *Governance, the Game* is a concrete alternative: an AI that compiles plain-language rules and **audits agent actions into a public, append-only verdict log**, inside a world whose governing power **resets on a cadence** and whose compute budget is **voted above a floor no majority can strip**. It is transparency-and-accountability machinery you can watch work — and it is honest about its single-provider dependency rather than papering over it.

**Body:**

**The alternative, framed as internet-health infrastructure.** The default trajectory for multi-agent AI is opaque enforcement on infrastructure one party controls. This project builds the opposite as a running exhibit: **legible rules, a public audit trail, and power that dissolves on a clock.**

- **Rule-aggregation as legible policy.** Rules are authored in natural language at five levels (charter → world → room → family → character). The aggregator compiles them into a per-context policy, treating every lower-level rule as **untrusted, delimited data** (levels are authored by rivals in the competition frame), passing the result through a **coded post-check** that strips anything the charter's floor forbids. Every time conflict-resolution voids an authored rule, the author is told — *interstitial lawmaking surfaced, not silent.*
- **The magistrate as an open auditing tool.** An AI magistrate runs **asynchronously over a signed, append-only action log** and writes reasoned public verdicts.
